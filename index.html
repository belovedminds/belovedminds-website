What dementia takes

Dementia doesn't just take memory. It takes the record of a person.

The names. The work they were proud of. The garden, the songs, the stories that tie a life together. As dementia advances, all of it slips away alongside the person — and in a community caring for many, no one can hold a whole lifetime for every resident. And yet those details are the very things that still land for someone living with dementia. Hearing their own life told back to them is one of the few things that reaches through the fog.

What we give back

Dementia works by taking things away. Beloved Minds gives them back.

None of this is for lack of heart. Families pour themselves in, caregivers give everything they have — the gaps that remain aren't gaps in effort, they're gaps in reach, the kind no amount of devotion can close alone. That is where Beloved Minds begins.

It makes the community where a resident lives the place that actively pushes against what dementia is doing, every single day.

Bella calls each resident by name, every day. The conversation carries itself when a resident cannot fully partake. Families decide how confusion is met, and at what level their loved one is engaged. Keepsakes are captured with the foresight of what may be lost — but needs to be kept.

Every other platform in this space delivers a feature. A call. A check-in. A notification.
This is something else entirely.

The questions directors ask

Fair questions, answered plainly

“Couldn't our own team do this?”

Your team already does the part that matters most — the care, the presence, the knowing. What no one can do is sit with every resident, every day, and pick up each conversation where it left off. Every resident is a whole life history, held across shifts and turnover. That isn't a shortcoming of any team. It's beyond what any person can hold. Bella holds it, and what she learns sits underneath the care your staff gives, and supports it.

“How is this different from a check-in service?”

A check-in call asks a yes-or-no question and logs the answer. For a resident with dementia, that's often a call they can't complete and won't remember. Beloved Minds is built for dementia and Alzheimer's alone — every call tailored to the individual, their history, their level, their family's wishes. No check-in service lets a family decide how confusion is handled. None captures a resident's history. Ours does both. The conversation is the point. The data comes out of it.

“Isn't this just an AI voice assistant?”

General-purpose AI is built for people who can keep up with it — ask, answer, move on. Point that at a resident who needs a moment to find a word and it fails. Every meaningful choice here is a memory-care decision: Cognitive Engagement Level, Confusion Response Mode, and the choice to remember, so nothing starts over.

What actually happens

One call. Everything it sets in motion.

Every day, Bella calls Janet by name. She asks about the year she was crowned rodeo queen, about the secret language her mother invented, about the singing bear at Disneyland whose song the family laughed about for decades — because she knows all of it. Janet laughs. She tells a story she has told before, and it is met as though for the first time. When she can't find a word, Bella waits. When she's confused, Bella agrees, because that is what her daughter asked for.

That is one call. Here is what it sets in motion — without anyone on your team lifting a finger.

How it's built

Every choice here is a memory-care decision

The four that define us

🎚️

Cognitive Engagement Level Patent pending

Every resident is set to one of five engagement levels. At Level 1, Bella asks open-ended questions and follows wherever the conversation goes. At Level 5, she carries nearly all of it, weaving a resident's own family memories into one continuous story — they may not say anything back, but the memory is still theirs, and hearing it can still bring them joy.

🧭

Confusion Response Mode Patent pending

Families choose how Bella responds when a resident is confused: agree, gently redirect, or offer a gentle correction. It's the single most consequential setting in memory care. Families make the call, and it's honored on every call — no resident is ever argued with or made to feel small.

📉

Priority Alerts Patent pending

The platform learns each resident's baseline, then alerts on three things: a drift across a series of calls, a sudden drop from one call to the next, or a trigger word in conversation. Confusion on its own is expected in memory care and never treated as an alarm. What reaches your team is what actually warrants attention.

📖

Story Cards Patent pending

Not every call produces one. But when a resident shares something that matters — a memory, a moment, something they'd want remembered — it becomes a formatted keepsake the family can keep and return to. The stories they tell, captured and preserved, so what they carry is never lost.

Mission & vision

Why we exist

Our Mission

To change what dementia is allowed to take.

It will take memory. We understand that. But it does not have to take a person's place in the world, their sense of being known, or the record of who they were. Those are losses the world has simply come to expect — and we don't.

Every day, for as long as we're needed.

Our Vision

A world where no one with dementia is left alone with what they can no longer hold. Where the details fade and someone is there to fill them in — to tell the story back, to say the names, to remember out loud what a person can no longer remember for themselves.

A world where a life keeps growing even as memory recedes. Where the stories that were never told are captured before they're gone, and a family who has been grieving someone still living is given something to hold when the time comes — not a record of decline, but a portrait of a whole person.

And a world where the people who do this work every day feel it. Where a facility isn't carrying one more thing, but is prouder of the care they give because of what we bring to it.
